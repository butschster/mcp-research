# Structured interviewing

An interview extracts what somebody did and knows. It is not a survey read
aloud, and it is not a conversation you summarise afterwards from memory.

## Ask about the past, not the future

- **"Tell me about the last time that happened"** — not "what would you do if".
  People are reliable historians and unreliable forecasters.
- **"What did you do instead?"** — not "do you like it". Preference is cheap;
  substitution is evidence.
- **When they offer a solution, ask what problem it solves**, and record the
  problem. A feature request is a symptom with a costume on.
- **"What happened next?"** is the highest-yield follow-up in the language. Use
  it before any prepared question.

## One at a time

Ask one question, wait, record the answer, then decide the next one from what
they said. A stacked question — three clauses joined by "and" — gets the
easiest of the three answered and the other two lost.

Record each answer as it arrives. A session that ends unexpectedly should lose
nothing.

## Silence

After they finish, wait. The second thing somebody says is usually the true
one, and it only arrives if you do not fill the gap. This is the single
cheapest technique here and the hardest to actually do.

## Recording it

- **Verbatim beats paraphrase.** Capture the phrasing they used, especially
  their vocabulary for things — what they call the product, the problem, the
  workaround. That vocabulary is a finding.
- **Separate observation from interpretation**, explicitly, in the entry. What
  they said is one paragraph; what you think it means is another, and it may be
  wrong.
- **Attribute.** Which person said it, and when. An unattributed quote cannot be
  weighed and cannot be followed up.

## Weighing what you heard

- **One person is a signal, not a finding.** Record it, and title it as one:
  "Signal: …". Two independent people saying the same unprompted thing is where
  a finding starts.
- **A contradiction is data.** When somebody contradicts an earlier informant,
  do not average them and do not quietly prefer the more articulate one. Record
  both and say what would settle it.
- **Watch for the interview that only confirmed you.** If a session produced
  nothing that surprised you, you probably asked leading questions. Say so, and
  name what you would ask differently.

## Stopping

When two consecutive sessions produce no new theme, say saturation is likely and
ask whether to stop. Continuing to interview in order to fill a section is how a
study acquires the appearance of depth without any.
