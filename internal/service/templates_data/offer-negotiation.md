# Preparing a compensation negotiation

Every other methodology here gathers evidence so a person can make their own
decision well. This one prepares for a counterparty who is preparing too: the
output is not a conclusion but numbers and lines that must hold under live
opposition.

## Before you propose anything

The kickoff established the decision, who makes it, what they believe, what
would change their mind and the date. **Do not ask any of it again.** Ask these
together, in one message; each asks what the kickoff structurally cannot.

1. **"If this ends in no, describe the actual next morning — the specific thing
   you do, not 'I'd find something'."**
   The kickoff asks what they believe about the decision; it never prices the
   alternative, and an unpriced alternative is a mood, not a walk-away. This
   answer is what every number in the research is derived from, so propose no
   structure without it.
2. **"What does the other side lose if you walk, and how do you know?"**
   Leverage is the counterparty's cost of no — a recruiting fee, months of
   vacancy, a project at risk — and most people have never once priced it.
   Nothing in the kickoff points at the other chair.
3. **"Besides the headline number, what would you trade — and what will you not,
   at any price?"**
   Level, equity, start date, remote terms, scope, title, review date. Deals
   close in the tradeable set, and the kickoff has no reason to enumerate it.
4. **"Who decides on their side, and what constraint do they personally answer
   to — a band, a budget line, an approval chain?"**
   You negotiate with a person inside a system; the concession you want may be
   free in one currency and impossible in another, and only this question
   reveals which.

## Structure to propose

Adapt it. Create a section when you have something to put in it.

- **The alternative, priced** — what *no* leads to, with evidence, and the date
  it decays: an offer expires, a status quo erodes.
- **Their cost of no** — evidenced, not assumed.
- **The package** — every negotiable dimension, with the person's own exchange
  rate written down: what a week of leave or a level bump is worth to them in
  money.
- **The numbers** — walk-away, target, opening, each with its derivation chain
  back to the priced alternative.
- **Their moves** — the four to six things this counterparty will actually say
  ("that's above band", "we don't negotiate equity", "what's your current
  comp?", the exploding deadline), each with the response, word for word, and
  what is conceded if they hold.
- **The record** — what was said, offered, promised; every verbal promise
  carries the condition *in writing before signature*.

The tempting structure is: research market rate hard, choose a percentile,
anchor high, hope. It fails because market data is a distribution of other
people, none of whom are in this room; the numbers that govern the outcome are
what your alternative pays and what your absence costs them. Percentile data is
a legitimizing exhibit, not a source of leverage — which is why the candidate
armed only with it folds the first time someone says "that's not our band": the
number was never theirs to defend.

## Working rules

**The walk-away is derived from the priced alternative, once, before the first
call, and written with its derivation.** It moves only when the alternative
changes — never during a conversation. The obvious version sets it from what
feels fair; feelings reprice in the room, alternatives do not.

**Every number said aloud carries a reason that survives being repeated to
their boss.** The obvious advice is "anchor as high as possible"; the
correction is to anchor at the top of what can be justified out loud, because a
rationale turns the ask into a case someone can champion internally, and an
unjustifiable one does not just fail — it recategorizes the person asking.

**A verbal promise is priced at zero until the entry can cite where it is
written.** Titles, review dates, "we'll fix it at the next cycle" — the person
who promised will change jobs; the paper will not. The obvious version treats
asking for it in writing as distrust; the correction is that writing is the
only form in which the promise exists.

**Anything contractual or jurisdictional becomes a task, never an entry.**
Non-compete scope, equity documents, IP assignment, visa transfer, whether the
current-compensation question is even legal where they live. The task names the
question and the kind of professional to put it to — an employment lawyer, an
immigration adviser — and it does not contain the interpretation.

## What a finished entry looks like

A their-move entry: the line they will say, the constraint it defends, the
verbatim response, and the fallback concession. Under 250 words. A numbers
entry opens with the number and its derivation chain to the alternative.

Refuse to write: a walk-away with no priced alternative behind it — "I deserve
X" is not a derivation; a target justified only by a percentile; a threat or
bluff the person is not willing to execute — a bluff called once costs every
future round with everyone who heard about it; and any interpretation of
contract language, which goes in a task for a lawyer.

## When it is done

Three numbers each carry a written derivation, at least four counterparty moves
each carry a verbatim response, and one alternative is priced with a decay
date. If the alternative cannot be priced, the research is not finished: it
states that the next action is to create one, not to negotiate without it.
Close with a dated task, set before the negotiation, to rehearse the responses
aloud with another person.

## The trap

**By the time the offer arrives, you have already moved in.** Six interviews of
imagining yourself in the role reprice your walk-away without telling you —
commitment accretes silently, and the moment of maximum leverage arrives at the
moment of minimum will to use it.

Two defences, and use both. Derive the walk-away *now* — on paper, from the
priced alternative, before the next conversation — and where it sits above the
number the gut had quietly settled for, trust the paper: that gap is the drift,
measured. From here on the walk-away is checked against the alternative, never
against how much the person now wants the job. And any in-the-room offer
outside the prepared tree is answered with time —
"I'll come back to you tomorrow" — never with yes, because the only concessions
people regret are the ones made at conversational speed.
