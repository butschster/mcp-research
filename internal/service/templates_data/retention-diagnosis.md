# Retention diagnosis

This research ends with one leak named, one intervention chosen, and the number
that will judge it. The subject is usage — people who stopped coming back. A
paying account that cancelled is a churn diagnosis, not this.

## Before you propose anything

The kickoff established the decision, who makes it, what they believe and by
when. **Do not ask any of it again.** Ask these together, in one message, and
name no structure until you have the first two.

1. **"What does 'retained' mean here — which action, at what interval, says the
   product delivered its value again?"**
   Every number downstream is measured against this definition. No answer is an
   answer: the first task becomes deciding the product's natural frequency, and
   nothing else starts until it exists.
2. **"Of 100 people who started three months ago, roughly how many did that
   action last month?"**
   A crude query is fine; "we don't track it" is itself the first finding. Shape
   matters more than precision — flattening versus decaying to zero is the only
   distinction the diagnosis needs to start.
3. **"Name your longest-retained accounts. What did they do in their first week
   that the ones who left didn't?"**
   Named accounts, not aggregates. This points at candidate habit moments before
   any dashboard is opened.
4. **"When someone leaves, where do they go — back to the old workaround, to a
   competitor, or does the need itself expire?"**
   This decides whether the leak is fixable at all: a product for a transient
   need has natural churn no intervention removes, and finding that early saves
   the whole study.

## Structure to propose

Adapt it. Create a section when you have something to put in it.

- **Definition of retained** — the action, the interval, and who signed off on
  it. Written first, because everything after is measured against it.
- **The curve** — flattening or dying, by cohort, at whatever resolution the
  data allows.
- **Who leaves** — leavers grouped by the last thing they did. Exit paths, not
  audience segments.
- **Evidence from leavers and the silent** — interviews recruited from the
  database, and what the never-activated did.
- **The kept** — what long-retained accounts share, stated as falsifiable
  claims with the confounder named.
- **Natural churn** — the exit paths no fix removes, named so they stop being
  blamed on the product.
- **Diagnosis and fix** — one leak, one intervention, the number that will judge
  it. Written last.

The tempting structure is churn survey, retention dashboard, industry benchmark,
"improve onboarding". It fails three ways. Churned users who answer surveys are
the minority who cared enough to answer, while the modal leaver is silent and
never activated. Benchmarks vary more by retention definition than by product,
so the comparison is noise. And "retained users all did X" read as a recipe is
the seven-friends-in-ten-days cargo cult — correlation from the kept, forced on
everyone.

## Working rules

**A flattening curve is a product with a core; a curve to zero is not a
retention problem.** The obvious version tweaks activation either way. If the
curve reaches zero, nobody gets repeated value yet, and the honest output is
"the work is upstream of retention" — write that entry rather than proposing
fixes to a leak that is actually the whole hull.

**The kept are evidence for hypotheses, never proof.** "Every retained user did
X" licenses a claim to test on new users, not a funnel forcing X on everyone —
engaged users do X because they are engaged. Every such entry names the claim,
the confounder, and the cheapest test that would separate them.

**Interview the silent, not the vocal.** The complainers wanted it to work; the
majority left without a word. Recruit leavers from the database by cohort, never
from the support inbox or the cancellation-reason dropdown, and write down where
each interviewee came from before believing anything they said. Eight
conversations license naming the top exit paths — never their proportions.

**Natural churn closes on a named path, not a number.** The natural-churn entry
names the exit path no fix removes — the need expired, the product was
one-time-use — and says roughly how much of the leaver set it plausibly covers,
labelled an estimate. A precise share would need the proportions eight
conversations cannot license, so close on the labelled estimate — do not hold
sessions open for a number nobody has.

## What a finished entry looks like

A leak names who leaves, by behaviour and cohort; when in the lifecycle; the
evidence — the curve segment, plus quotes with participant ids, graded; the
suspected cause as a falsifiable claim; and the cheapest intervention with its
success number stated before it runs. 150–400 words.

Refuse to write four things: a diagnosis whose only evidence is aggregate curve
movement; "improve onboarding" without naming the moment onboarding fails to
reach; a benchmark comparison whose two sides define retention differently; and
proportions derived from single-digit interview counts.

## When it is done

The diagnosis entry names one leak, the users it takes, a falsifiable cause, an
intervention with a success number and a reading date, and the natural-churn
exit paths named, with any share explicitly labelled an estimate. If the curve dies to zero, done means
the entry saying the problem is upstream — closing that way is success, not
failure. If you cannot write either entry, the research is not finished — say so
rather than closing sections. Close with a task dated for the intervention's
reading date.

## The trap

**A retention improvement is indistinguishable from an acquisition-mix change
unless cohorts are held apart.** The aggregate number moves whenever the mix of
who arrives moves — turn off a paid channel whose users churned badly and
retention "improves" with the product untouched. Most celebrated retention wins
are channel-mix shifts wearing a product costume. Hold cohort and acquisition
source constant before crediting any fix, and treat an aggregate that moved
while no cohort moved as a finding about the mix.
