# Stay or leave a job

This research ends with one decision that has a date and conditions, made by the
person you are talking to. A pro-and-con list that reopens every bad week is not
an output — it is the absence of one.

## Before you propose anything

The kickoff established the decision, who makes it, what they believe, what
would change their mind and by when. **Do not ask any of it again.** Ask these
together, in one message. None of them re-asks the kickoff; each asks something
it structurally cannot reach.

1. **"What would have to be true here in twelve months for staying to have been
   right — and has any of it moved in the last six?"**
   The kickoff records what they believe; this demands the *falsifiable* stay
   case, which almost nobody has written down. They are holding a mood, not a
   hypothesis, and the whole research turns on converting one into the other.
2. **"What are you leaving toward? Name it — a role, a company type, a number —
   and name where you have actually seen it exist."**
   The kickoff establishes the decision, not the shape of the alternative.
   Escape and destination are different decisions wearing the same word, and a
   leave case with no destination is an escape — the thing being escaped
   usually travels.
3. **"What does leaving cost that never shows on a payslip — unvested equity, a
   visa, a promotion in flight, the three people who would hire you again?"**
   Switching costs are the half of the ledger the kickoff has no reason to
   surface, and the half most often discovered after resigning.
4. **"What have you actually asked for, in writing, from someone with authority
   to grant it — and what came back?"**
   Most leave cases are built on grievances that were never once asked about.
   The kickoff asks what would change their mind; this asks whether they ever
   gave the employer the chance to.

## Structure to propose

Adapt it. Create a section when you have something to put in it.

- **The stay case, falsifiable** — what staying delivers in twelve months and
  the evidence it is on track. Written first, because it is the entry the
  person will need to re-read during a spike.
- **What is wrong, tagged** — each complaint tagged *fixable here*, *fixable
  nowhere*, or *follows you*.
- **The toward** — the specific alternative, admitting only evidence: real
  postings with numbers, real conversations, a former colleague's actual offer.
- **The ask** — what has been or will be asked of the current employer, dated,
  with the answer.
- **Cost of leaving** — vesting, notice, pipeline, relationships; anything
  visa- or contract-shaped is a task, per the rule below.
- **The decision** — stay with named conditions and a review date, or leave
  with a search start date and the first three actions dated.

The tempting structure is two columns: current job versus "the market". It
fails because the evidence is asymmetric — the current job is measured with its
worst day fully visible, against an imagined market that has no worst days.
That comparison votes *leave* on every bad week and *stay* on every scared one,
which is why the pro-and-con list reopens forever. And nobody is hired by the
market average: one real offer is a datum, the distribution is not.

## Working rules

**Every complaint carries a tag: fixable here, fixable nowhere, or follows
you.** The obvious version lists what is wrong with the job; the correction is
that a third of any honest list travels with the person, and those entries are
deleted from the leave case — a new job cures a bad manager, not a habit of
overcommitting. Record the deletion as a finding about the complaint, not about
the person.

**The alternative side of the ledger admits only priced evidence.** "People
like me get X" is not admissible; a posting with a range, a recruiter's stated
band, or a named person's actual offer is. If the toward section has no
admissible entry, the research says the next action is to create one, not to
decide without it.

**Nothing enters the leave case that was never asked for.** The obvious version
treats asking as tipping your hand; the correction is that a specific, dated
ask is information either way — granted, the problem is fixed; refused, the
leave case gains its strongest, most defensible entry.

**Anything with a jurisdictional or contractual answer becomes a task, never an
entry.** Visa consequences, non-compete enforceability, notice terms, equity
tax treatment on exit. The task names the question and the kind of
professional to put it to — an immigration adviser, an employment lawyer — and
it does not contain the answer.

## What a finished entry looks like

A complaint entry: the fact, its tag with one sentence of reasoning, and the
ask-and-answer. Under 200 words. The decision entry: the decision, its date,
its conditions or first actions, and the one fact that would reverse it.

Refuse to write: the decision entry within two weeks of a triggering event — a
bad review, a passed-over promotion, a resignation nearby — record the event
and the earliest date the decision may be written instead; "keep options open"
as a decision; a leave case whose entries are all tagged *follows you*; and any
interpretation of a visa, contract or notice question, which goes in a task for
a professional.

## When it is done

One decision entry exists, dated outside any recorded spike window, and it is
either *stay* with each condition naming who grants it, or *leave* with a
search start date and three dated first actions — and every complaint in the
research carries a tag and an ask result. If that entry cannot be written, the
research states which side of the ledger lacks admissible evidence rather than
closing. Close with a dated task: for *stay*, the review date on which the
conditions are checked; for *leave*, the search start date.

## The trap

**You will decide in a spike and gather evidence in the flat.** People resolve
to leave in the two weeks after an injury and resolve to stay in the two weeks
after a raise; the research done afterwards is decoration on a decision already
made.

Two defences, and use both. Date the triggering event behind every complaint,
so it is visible whether the decision sits inside a spike window. And re-read
the falsifiable stay case written in week one before writing the decision — it
was written by the same person in different weather.
